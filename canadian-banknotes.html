Canadian banknote serial numbers: what makes a note collectible

Almost every Canadian note is worth its face value. A few are worth more to collectors because of the serial number printed on them.

This page explains how Canadian serials work and what collectors look for. Picave checks each note for you.

Covers Bank of Canada notes from the 1986 Birds of Canada series to today's polymer notes. Earlier series are not covered.

Checking a pile of old notes

Check each one before spending or depositing it. The $2 note is no longer legal tender, but the Bank of Canada says it keeps its face value and can be redeemed through a financial institution or the Bank of Canada.

  1. Keep notes that were stored together in their original order. Consecutive serial numbers can form a run, which collectors look at as a set.
  2. Scan each note with Picave, or type its serial and choose its denomination. Picave shows the note's series year and signatures, with badges for pattern serials, first and last prefixes and replacement notes.
  3. Grade the notes that stand out. Picave's grading guide asks a short set of questions about the note's condition, with separate questions for paper and polymer, then shows whether the note is collectible at that grade.
  4. Show a specialist any note Picave marks as a Collector Issue. These have features that need hands-on expertise to confirm.

How Canadian serial numbers work

Every note from 1986 on has a serial number of three letters and seven digits.

Paper notes: Birds of Canada and Canadian Journey

AIKPrefix 4182736Number

Three letters and seven digits, as on this made-up 1991 $20. The year on the front is the series year, and notes kept being printed with it for years afterwards under later signatures.

Polymer notes, from 2011

BSCPrefix 4182736Number

The same three letters and seven digits, as on this made-up 2012 $20.

Replacement notes

AIXPrefix 0418273Number

On Birds of Canada notes, a replacement note has X as the third letter of its prefix, as on this made-up 1991 $20. Replacement notes were printed to take the place of faulty notes found during printing.

What collectors look for

Condition comes first. Picave's catalogue records the lowest grade at which each note attracts collectors, and for almost every Canadian note that is Extremely Fine. Once a note is graded, Picave shows whether it is collectible at that grade, or the grade it would need.

Pattern serials

A serial whose seven digits form a pattern is the best-known kind of collectible. These examples are made up, using a real prefix.

Radar

BSC 3579753

Reads the same forwards and backwards.

Solid

BSC 7777777

Every digit is the same. Rare.

Ladder

BSC 1234567

The digits climb or fall one step at a time.

Binary

BSC 1611611

The number uses only two different digits.

Low serial

BSC 0000007

A number from 1 to 100.

Picave checks the seven digits, and it also flags near patterns, one digit away from a radar, ladder or binary. With an odd number of digits, a Canadian serial cannot be a repeater, which needs one half to repeat the other. Bookends are collected on US notes but are not counted as a pattern on Canadian notes.

First and last prefixes

Each run of notes starts at a first prefix and ends at a last prefix, and collectors look for both. They differ from note to note and from one signature pair to the next. Picave marks them with a First Prefix or Last Prefix badge.

Replacement notes

Picave knows which prefixes and serial ranges were used for replacement notes, marks them with a Star Note badge and treats them as rare.

Signatures

Canadian notes carry two signatures, and the pair changed many times: 13 pairs appear from 1986 to today. Picave shows the signatures of every note it identifies.

Notes that need a specialist

A few transition notes, printed as one design gave way to the next, have details a serial number cannot settle. Picave marks these as a Collector Issue and suggests a specialist.

Why identification can be tricky

A serial number alone does not always point to one note. These are the cases that catch collectors out, and how Picave handles each.

Changeover prefixes

When the signatures changed, the new pair often started part way through a prefix. Notes below the changeover carry the earlier pair and notes above it the later one, so the seven digits decide. Picave tells them apart by the number.

A prefix used in more than one series

Some prefixes were used again in a later series. When a serial fits notes from more than one series, Picave asks for the year printed on the front of your note, then shows the signatures for that year so you can pick yours.

A series year is not a printing year

The year on the front is the series year, and the same year kept being printed for years. Picave shows the year printed on your note first, with the years that note was produced underneath.

Questions

Are old Canadian banknotes worth anything?

Most are worth their face value. The $2 note is no longer legal tender, but the Bank of Canada says it keeps its face value and can be redeemed. A few notes are worth more to collectors, such as replacement notes, first and last prefixes and pattern serials, especially in good condition. Check each one with Picave before spending or depositing it.

Is my $2 bill worth anything?

Most $2 notes are worth $2, and although the $2 is no longer legal tender, the Bank of Canada says it keeps its face value and can be redeemed through a financial institution or the Bank of Canada. A $2 note attracts collectors when it is a replacement note, a first or last prefix or a pattern serial, and is in good condition.

What is a replacement note?

A replacement note takes the place of a faulty note found during printing. On Birds of Canada notes, a replacement note has X as the third letter of its prefix, as in AIX 0418273. Picave knows the replacement prefixes and serial ranges and treats these notes as rare.

What does the year on my note mean?

It is the series year. Notes kept being printed with the same year for years afterwards, under later signatures, so Picave shows the years a note was produced as well as the year printed on it.

What is a changeover prefix?

A prefix where the signatures changed part way through. Notes below the changeover carry the earlier pair and notes above it the later pair, so the serial number decides which you have. Picave checks the number to tell them apart.

What is a radar note?

A radar serial reads the same forwards and backwards, like 3579753. Picave checks the seven digits for radars and the other patterns collectors look for.
