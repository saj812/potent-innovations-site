Euro banknote serial numbers: what makes a note collectible

Almost every euro note is worth its face value. A few are worth more to collectors because of the serial number printed on them.

This page explains how euro serials work and what collectors look for. Picave checks each note for you.

Covers euro notes of the first series, from 2002, and the Europa series, €5 to €500.

Checking a pile of old notes

Check each one before spending or depositing it. Older euro notes keep their value: the European Central Bank says first series notes will always retain their value and can be exchanged for an unlimited period of time at the Eurosystem's national central banks.

  1. Keep notes that were stored together in their original order. Consecutive serial numbers can form a run, which collectors look at as a set.
  2. Scan each note with Picave, or type its serial and choose its denomination. Picave shows the note's series and possible signatures, with badges for pattern serials and first and last prefixes.
  3. Grade the notes that stand out. Picave's grading guide asks a short set of questions about the note's condition, then shows whether the note is collectible at that grade.
  4. Show a specialist any note Picave marks as a Collector Issue. These have features that need hands-on expertise to confirm.

How euro serial numbers work

Every euro serial has twelve characters, but the two series use them differently.

Europa series

UPrinter ALetter 5903396364Number

Two letters and ten digits, as on this made-up €20. The European Central Bank says the first letter identifies the printing works, and the second letter has no particular meaning: it simply makes more serial numbers possible.

First series, from 2002

SBank 09995004610Number

One letter and eleven digits, as on this made-up €20. The letter identifies the central bank the note was printed for: S is the Banca d'Italia, the European Central Bank's own example.

The last digit

The last digit of every euro serial is a check digit, worked out from the letters and the other digits. Every example on this page passes it. Picave uses it to work out the serials missing from a run.

What collectors look for

Condition comes first. Picave's catalogue records the lowest grade at which each note attracts collectors, and for almost every euro note that is Extremely Fine. Once a note is graded, Picave shows whether it is collectible at that grade, or the grade it would need.

Pattern serials

A serial whose digits form a pattern is the best-known kind of collectible. These examples are made up, using real printing works letters, and each one passes the check digit.

Radar

UC 1355995531

Reads the same forwards and backwards.

Repeater

UA 3722137221

The first half of the number repeats in the second half.

Solid

UA 3333333333

Every digit is the same. Rare.

Ladder

UD 0123456789

The digits climb or fall one step at a time.

Binary

UA 1111611666

The number uses only two different digits.

Low serial

UC 0000000028

A number from 1 to 100.

Picave checks all the digits after the letters, and it also flags near patterns, one digit away from a radar, ladder or binary. Bookends are collected on US notes but are not counted as a pattern on euro notes.

First and last prefixes

Collectors look for the first and last prefixes of a run. Picave marks them with a First Prefix or Last Prefix badge.

Signatures

Euro notes carry the signature of the President of the European Central Bank, and four Presidents have signed them: Duisenberg, Trichet, Draghi and Lagarde. Which one a note carries cannot be worked out from its serial number, so Picave shows the signatures your serial could carry for you to compare.

The €500

The €500 was issued only in the first series. The European Central Bank has stopped producing it, but says the first series €500 remains legal tender.

Notes that need a specialist

A few notes have details a serial number cannot settle. Picave marks these as a Collector Issue and suggests a specialist.

Why identification can be tricky

A serial number alone does not always point to one note. These are the cases that catch collectors out, and how Picave handles each.

The signature is not in the serial

The same serial can carry different signatures depending on when the note was printed. Picave shows each possible signature, so you can match the one on your note.

One letter, two meanings

On first series notes the letter is the central bank the note was printed for. On Europa notes the first letter is the printing works, so it does not say which country issued the note.

Letters that look like digits

A Europa serial's second letter can be O or I, which look like the digits 0 and 1. If a serial does not match anything as it was read, Picave also tries reading that character the other way.

Questions

Are old euro banknotes still valid?

Yes. The European Central Bank says first series notes will always retain their value and can be exchanged for an unlimited period of time at the Eurosystem's national central banks. A few are worth more to collectors, such as pattern serials and first and last prefixes, especially in good condition. Check each one with Picave first.

What does the first letter of a euro serial mean?

On Europa series notes, the European Central Bank says the first letter identifies the printing works, and the second letter has no particular meaning. On first series notes, the letter identifies the central bank the note was printed for, such as S for the Banca d'Italia.

Is the €500 note still legal tender?

Yes. The European Central Bank has stopped producing the €500, but says the first series €500 remains legal tender. Like any note, a €500 attracts collectors when it has a pattern serial or a first or last prefix and is in good condition.

Which signature does my euro note carry?

It depends on when the note was printed, which the serial number does not show. Picave shows the signatures your serial could carry, so you can compare them with the one on your note.

What is a radar note?

A radar serial reads the same forwards and backwards, like 1355995531. Picave checks all the digits after the letters for radars and the other patterns collectors look for.

What is the last digit of a euro serial?

It is a check digit, worked out from the letters and the other digits of the serial. Picave uses it to work out the serial numbers missing from a run.
